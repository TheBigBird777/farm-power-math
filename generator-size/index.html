How this calculator works

Motors (well pumps, compressors in refrigerators and freezers, air conditioners) need a short burst of extra power to start, often two to three times their running watts. A generator has to cover everything that's already running plus the single biggest starting surge. The calculator adds the running watts of every load you've switched on, adds the largest extra starting draw, and recommends a running rating with the headroom you choose.

Where to find the watts

Check each appliance's nameplate or manual. If it lists amps, multiply by the voltage (amps × 120 V or 240 V). Starting watts for well pumps are often listed by the pump maker; if not, plan on two to three times running watts.

Well pumps and 240 V

Many well pumps run on 240 V and need a generator with a 240 V outlet, plus a proper transfer switch or interlock. Never back-feed a house through a dryer outlet: it can electrocute utility workers.

Stagger the starts

The math assumes only one motor starts at a time. Turning big loads on one by one lets a smaller generator do the job.
